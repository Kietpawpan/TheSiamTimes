Behind Rolex's Slogan: “A Crown for Every Achievement”
The five points of Rolex's famous logo, and the strengths that earned each one
A Rolex Oyster Perpetual. Photo: EMore98

Look closely at the Rolex logo and you'll count five points on its crown. As I see it, each point stands for an achievement that has kept the brand at the top of the watch world for more than a century.

The first is the name itself. When founder Hans Wilsdorf registered "Rolex" in 1908, he wanted a word that was short, looked elegant on a dial, and was easy to pronounce in any language. More than a hundred years later, it remains one of the most recognizable brand names on earth.

The second is Oystersteel. The Oyster case, introduced in 1926 as one of the world's first waterproof wristwatch cases, built Rolex's reputation for toughness. Today its steel watches are made from Oystersteel, a highly corrosion-resistant alloy that keeps its shine through years of daily wear.

The third is the Superlative Chronometer certification. Every Rolex is tested after it is fully assembled and must keep time to within two seconds a day, a standard stricter than the official Swiss chronometer requirement.

The fourth is the "Swiss Made" label, a promise of origin and quality that Rolex has upheld since moving its base to Geneva after the First World War.

The fifth is elegant design paired with fine craftsmanship. Rolex changes its models slowly and deliberately, so a watch bought today will still look right decades from now.

Together, these five achievements explain why the crown fits. Rolex didn't simply borrow a symbol of royalty; it earned one, point by point.
