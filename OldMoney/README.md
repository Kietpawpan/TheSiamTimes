# Old Money Atelier – web game

A dress-up game in the "old money" quiet-luxury style. Choose a lady or a gentleman, dress them in cashmere, tweed, silk and pearls for six society invitations, and The Siam Times style desk scores the look. All text in the game is in English.

Everything is in `GA-oldmoney.html`. There is no build step: open the file in a browser. On the site it is at https://thesiamtimes.com/OldMoney/GA-oldmoney.html. It works offline; only the Newsreader text font comes from Google Fonts, and the page falls back to Georgia without it.

## Playing

- **Lady / Gentleman** (top of the page) switches between the two models. Each keeps their own outfit, so you can switch back and forth.
- **Couple view** (on by default) shows the gentleman and the lady side by side, as in a country-house portrait. The one you are dressing stands in front with a brass ring at their feet; tap the other to dress them instead. **Valet's pick** dresses both, and **Present the look** judges the one you are dressing; the verdict also says how the other one is doing, with a button to present them instead. Turn Couple view off to see one model, larger.
- **Wardrobe tabs**: Model (skin tone, ten hairstyles for the lady and eight for the gentleman, hair colour including salt and pepper, eye colour, lipstick for the lady, and clean-shaven, stubble or a short beard for the gentleman), Tops, Dresses, Bottoms, Outerwear, Shoes and Accessories. Blazers and overcoats are named Brass-button blazer and Wool overcoat because they come in several colours. Country-house pieces include, for the gentleman, a tattersall shirt, a silk tie, a six-button waistcoat, chinos and flannels with turn-ups, Oxford brogues and a walking cane; and for the lady, a check hacking jacket, a tartan midi skirt, a tucked cashmere turtleneck, a gold brooch and a coiffed bob. The gentleman has no Dresses tab. His wardrobe includes black tie (evening shirt and bow tie, evening trousers, dinner jacket, patent oxfords), a repp tie, pocket square, signet ring, flat cap and Panama hat. Loafers, riding boots, boat shoes, plimsolls, sunglasses and the trap pieces are shared. Tap a piece to put it on, and tap it again to take it off. The colour swatches above the grid recolour the selected piece.
- A dress replaces the top and bottom. Shirts and blouses are tucked in; knits and the Breton top sit over the waistband.
- **The Season**: six invitations: polo, regatta, country weekend, garden party, club tennis and the opera. Each has its own scene. **Present the look** gives one to five stars. Your best stars and best score for each invitation are saved, and the invitation card keeps the tally out of 30 stars. An invitation where you have scored 100 gets a brass ring.
- **Free styling**: choose any of seven scenes. **Ask the style desk** tells you which invitation your look suits best.
- **Valet's pick** lays out the clothes and the hair for the current occasion and leaves the finishing touches (a watch, a scarf, the jewellery) to you. A valet look scores four stars; the fifth is yours to win. On a first visit the couple arrive fully dressed, accessories included.
- **Undo** (or Ctrl+Z) takes back the last change, including a Valet's pick or a Start over. It remembers the last 60 steps, for both models.
- **Save look** adds the look to the **Lookbook** (up to 24 looks). **Download portrait** saves a PNG of the model in the scene.
- **By your own hand**: a look that scores 100 with no help from the valet or from the stylist's Apply earns a seal for that invitation (press Start over and dress the model yourself). The invitation's dot turns brass, and the tally counts your seals. Six seals are the most the Season has to give.
- **On a phone** the stage stays at the top of the screen while you scroll the wardrobe, and the picture is cropped to the figures so they stay large. The buttons sit in one row that swipes sideways, with Present the look fixed at its end. The colours of the piece you tapped, and its Take off button, are in a bar at the bottom of the screen.
- **Outerwear** starts with **No outer layer**, and every tab has a **Take off** button beside the colours of the piece you have selected.
- **Accessories** are grouped: watches, glasses, belts, jewellery, ties and scarves, bags, hats, and the rest. Watches, glasses and belts are worn one at a time.
- **Hairstyles.** The lady: low chignon, sleek ponytail, blowout waves, classic bob, coiffed bob, French twist, Old Hollywood waves, pixie crop, sleek long hair, side braid. The gentleman: swept side part, swept back, tousled, short crop, slicked back, prep flow, curly crop, Ivy League cut.
- **Glasses**: round tortoiseshell sunglasses, round wire glasses, horn-rimmed glasses, browline glasses and aviator sunglasses for both; cat-eye and oversized sunglasses for the lady; and a logo shield trap. Clear-lens glasses suit any occasion; sunglasses belong outdoors, never at the opera.
- **Belts**, shared and drawn at either model's waist: smooth leather (any occasion), braided leather (regatta, polo, country), ribbon belt (regatta, tennis, garden party, polo), needlepoint (garden party, polo, regatta), suede (country, garden party), and a logo buckle trap.
- **Watches** (in Accessories), one at a time; choosing another replaces the one you wear. Each wrist watch fits either model and is drawn on the right wrist:

| Watch | Colours | Right for |
|---|---|---|
| Round dress watch | gold, silver case on leather | any occasion; best at the garden party and the opera |
| Rectangular dress watch | gold, silver case on leather | any occasion; best at the polo, the garden party and the opera |
| Bracelet watch | gold, silver | polo, garden party, opera |
| Steel sports watch | navy, black, bottle-green dial | regatta, tennis, polo, country |
| Diver's watch | navy, black, bottle-green bezel | regatta |
| Field watch | olive, navy, chocolate canvas strap | country, polo |
| Pocket watch (gentleman) | gold, silver chain across the waistcoat | country, garden party, opera |
| Iced-out watch | gold, set with diamonds | nowhere: a trap that costs quietness points |

- The **Quiet luxury** meter updates live as you dress.
- **Style guide** (top of the page) is a stylist with three sections:
  - **For this look**: the few changes that would most improve the current look for the chosen invitation, each with its reason and the points it adds. **Apply** changes only the piece that tip names (a dress tip names the top and bottom it replaces). The list then stays as it is: the applied tip is marked Applied with an **Undo** that puts just that piece back, the other tips keep their place with their points refreshed, and a tip that your other changes made unnecessary is greyed out. **New suggestions** gives a fresh list; so does changing the outfit by hand. The stylist tries every swap, colour change, accessory and hairstyle it could suggest and scores each one with the style desk's own rules, so its advice always raises the score. When the model has neither a top nor a bottom on, it offers the two together, and it can exchange one accessory for another, so it never runs out of advice before the look is finished. It offers one change for each finishing problem (the belt in another colour, not the belt and the shoes), and equal ideas take turns, so New suggestions shows other ways. The same advice appears under **How to improve** on the style desk's verdict.
  - **Dress code**: the key pieces for the lady and the gentleman at each invitation, with a point of etiquette. Tap a piece to put it on in the colour that scores best.
  - **The rules**: ten rules of quiet luxury that also hold off the screen (fit, neutrals, cloth, no logos, three accessories, dressing for the place, care, buying less and better, grooming, ease).
- **Kingsmere Hall** (the country invitation) is an English country house in autumn: an old oak, ivy on red brick, a gravel sweep with fallen leaves and a vintage saloon on the forecourt.
- The stage has an oil-on-canvas finish: brush streaks, canvas weave and a soft vignette laid over the scene in soft-light, also carried into the downloaded portrait.
- Every scene has a **The Siam Times** sign in Chomsky beside the model: a hanging plaque in the atelier, a pitch-side board at the polo, a dock sign at the harbour, an oak estate sign in the country, a stone plaque on the quad, a club board on the tennis windscreen, and a gilt easel at the opera.

## Scoring (out of 100)

| Part | Points | What counts |
|---|---|---|
| A complete outfit | 15 | A dress, or a top with a bottom (10), and shoes (5) |
| Right for the occasion | 40 | Each piece is tagged with the occasions it suits; accessories and hair count half. Accessories that suit any occasion (a watch, pearl studs, a belt) never count against the occasion score. Club tennis needs whites. The opera needs a gown and hair up (a low chignon or a French twist) for the lady, and black tie for the gentleman |
| Quietness | 20 | Logo or trap pieces (hoodie, chunky trainers, logo belt bag, chain stack) cost heavily, as do more than three accessories, more than two accent colours, and loud colours |
| Palette | 10 | Share of neutral colours (navy, cream, camel, white, charcoal and similar) |
| Finishing touches | 15 | At least one good accessory (without one, 4 points at most), and everything goes together: each rule broken in the list below costs 6 |

Quietness and palette are judged on what is actually worn: with no shoes they count for two thirds, and with only shoes for one third. A bare model scores nothing.

90 or more is five stars (Generational Wealth); 78 is four, 62 three and 45 two. 100 is marked Flawless.

### What goes together

The style desk names each rule broken in its verdict, and the stylist offers the change that mends it.

- **Leathers match**: brown with brown (chocolate, chestnut, camel), black with black, across the shoes, the leather belts and the top-handle bag. Burgundy, navy, cream and white go with either.
- **Metals match**: gold with gold, silver with silver, across the watch, metal-framed glasses, the brooch and the signet ring. Pearls and diamonds go with either.
- **Riding breeches** are worn with riding boots.
- **A tie** needs a collared shirt (the Oxford or the tattersall), one tie at a time. The evening shirt already has its bow tie.
- **A waistcoat** goes over a collared shirt or the evening shirt, not a knit.
- **A pocket square** needs a jacket: the blazer, the tweed jacket, the dinner jacket or the overcoat.
- **A pocket watch** is worn with the waistcoat, or with the dinner jacket.
- **Opera gloves** go with the gown.
- **A sweater over the shoulders** does not go on top of an overcoat or a trench coat.

Saved looks, the current outfit, the mode, your best results and your seals are kept in the browser's local storage.

## How it works

- The two models and every garment are inline SVG in a 400 × 820 box. Each garment is drawn from point lists that are smoothed into Bézier curves and mirrored for the two sides. Fabrics such as cable knit, herringbone tweed, quilting, corduroy, Breton stripes, florals and silk sheen are SVG patterns made for the chosen colour.
- **Proportions** are close to life: the models stand about 7.5 heads tall, with shoulders about 1.75 head heights across for the lady and 1.9 for the gentleman. Every piece is drawn on a slim fashion-plate figure, and at render time one mapping scales the head (with hair, hats, glasses and earrings) evenly while another widens the body and everything worn on it, so all the clothes keep fitting. Buttons, pearls, watch faces, lettering and fabric patterns are counter-scaled so they stay round and undistorted, and feet and shoes are narrowed about each foot. The head is drawn above the clothes, so collars and roll necks tuck under the chin.
- For a more lifelike look, every skin and garment shape gets a rounded light-and-shade gradient, and every garment layer casts a soft drop shadow on the layers under it. Faces have shaded eyelids, irises with a catchlight, a shaded nose, two-tone lips and soft cheek contours. Hands have a thumb and fingers, legs have knees and ankles, and hair is shaded from crown to ends.
- Each piece is marked for the lady, the gentleman or both (`who`), and has a layer number, so tucked shirts sit under trousers and boots sit over them.
- The scenes are drawn wider than the figure, so wide screens show more of them.
- The result chime uses Web Audio; there are no sound files. The stars arrive one at a time and the score counts up, unless the device asks for reduced motion.
- Below 900 px the page is one column. The stage column is sticky, offset by the height of the invitation, so the header and the invitation scroll away and the stage, the buttons and the wardrobe tabs stay. The stage is 42% of the screen height, and its picture is cropped to the figures.
- Every change goes through one function, which keeps the previous state of both looks for Undo.
- An open dialog keeps the keyboard focus inside it and gives the focus back when it closes.

## Font

The masthead and the scene signs use **Chomsky** by Fredrick R. Brennan, under the SIL Open Font License 1.1 (`Chomsky-LICENSE.txt`, which must stay with the game). The page embeds the same Latin-only WOFF2 subset as `Darts/GA-darts.html`; see `Darts/README.md` for how to rebuild it. The downloaded portrait carries the same font inside its SVG, so the sign keeps its lettering in the PNG.
