Thai Environmental Regulators Tighten Oversight on Livestock Pollution in Surin Province
Following reports of contaminated waterways, a multi-agency task force has mandated structural improvements at a large-scale pig farm to prevent environmental degradation.
Investigators from the Regional Environment and Pollution Office inspect the drainage infrastructure of a commercial livestock facility in the Sikhoraphum district of Surin province on Sept. 22.

SURIN, Thailand — Responding to mounting public concern over the integrity of local water sources, Thai environmental officials have launched a comprehensive investigation into a commercial pig farm in the Truem subdistrict of Surin’s Sikhoraphum district. The move, led by Montri Kiatphaophan, director of the Regional Environment and Pollution Office 11, marks a significant step in the province's efforts to balance industrial agricultural growth with environmental preservation.

The inspection, conducted on Sept. 22, involved a coordinated task force representing the provincial offices of livestock, public health, and natural resources, alongside local administrators from the Truem Subdistrict Administrative Organization. The operation was prompted by formal complaints from residents who alleged that the facility was discharging untreated wastewater into public waterways, threatening the local ecosystem.

Upon arrival, investigators found a facility housing approximately 1,600 hogs, a scale that classifies the operation as a "Category B" pollution source under the 1992 Enhancement and Conservation of National Environmental Quality Act. While officials reported that no active wastewater discharge was observed during the time of the inspection, the structural risks posed by the current management system were deemed sufficient to warrant immediate regulatory action.

Under the authority of the Public Health Act of 1992, local administrators are set to issue an administrative order aimed at preventing public nuisances and mitigating future environmental risks. The farm’s operators have been instructed to develop and submit a comprehensive plan to modernize their wastewater treatment systems and reinforce "at-risk" points within the facility to prevent accidental leaks.

The Regional Environment and Pollution Office emphasized that these measures are part of a broader strategy to ensure commercial agriculture adheres to strict environmental standards. Officials confirmed that they would maintain an active monitoring presence in the region to ensure the facility remains in compliance and that the local water supply is protected from further contamination.
