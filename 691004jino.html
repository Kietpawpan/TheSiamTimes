The Ascent of Atthaya Thitikul, Golf’s Unrelenting Star
Shattering records since she was 14, the Thai prodigy affectionately known as Jeeno has cemented her status as the sport's dominant force, reclaiming the world No. 1 ranking with historically low scores and back-to-back multimillion-dollar victories.

Long before she was hoisting multimillion-dollar checks and etching her name into the bedrock of the LPGA Tour’s record books, Atthaya Thitikul was a 14-year-old amateur with an impossibly smooth swing and a preternatural calm.

In July 2017, at just 14 years, 4 months, and 19 days old, the golfer affectionately known as Jeeno captured the Ladies European Thailand Championship. The victory made her the youngest player to ever win a professional golf tournament—a distinction she held until 2023—and heralded the arrival of a generational talent. By the summer of 2019, she had ascended to the pinnacle of the amateur game, spending a total of 12 weeks as the world’s top-ranked amateur.

But it was her transition to the professional ranks that truly showcased the depth of her prowess. Thitikul broke through on the United States-based LPGA Tour in March 2022 with a victory at the JTBC Classic. By September of that year, the 19-year-old phenom had already amassed two LPGA victories and four titles on the Ladies European Tour. Her meteoric rise culminated that Halloween, when she officially became the No. 1 golfer in the Women’s World Golf Rankings, capping off a season that earned her LPGA Tour Rookie of the Year honors and establishing her as the fastest-rising star in the sport.

Rather than buckling under the weight of early expectations, Thitikul has only accelerated. The closing months of the 2024 and 2025 seasons served as a definitive coronation. In both years, she triumphed at the highly competitive season-ending CME Group Tour Championship, securing a staggering $4 million prize each time.

Her 2025 campaign, in particular, was a masterclass in consistency and execution. She topped the LPGA money list and secured her second Vare Trophy—having first won it in 2023—by posting a 68.68 scoring average, the lowest in the history of the tour. By August 2025, she had rightfully reclaimed her throne as the No. 1 player in the world.

For Thitikul, the prodigy who once dazzled the golf world as a teenager, the narrative is no longer just about how fast she arrived. It is about how thoroughly she intends to stay.
