A Silent Threat in the Trash: Thai Officials Urge Residents to Separate Electronic Waste
Environmental authorities in northeastern Thailand warn that discarded electronics are leaking toxic substances into soil and water, and they are asking the public to dispose of old devices safely
The director of Pollution Control Office Region 11 meets with officials in Nakhon Ratchasima to plan how to manage electronic waste across the lower northeastern region.

NAKHON RATCHASIMA, Thailand — Thailand's Environmental and Pollution Control Office Region 11 issued a public warning this week about the growing hazards of electronic waste, and it is pressing residents to keep discarded appliances and electronics out of the ordinary garbage stream to prevent toxic substances from seeping into the environment.

Dr. Montri Kiatphaophan, the office's director, said that electronic waste, known internationally as WEEE, has become a threat that demands urgent attention across the four provinces under the agency's watch: Nakhon Ratchasima, Chaiyaphum, Buriram and Surin. Such waste contains heavy metals and other hazardous materials, he said. When it is thrown out with household trash, or handed to untrained people who dismantle it improperly, poisons can contaminate soil and water sources, with direct consequences for public health.

To address the problem at its source, the office is asking residents to change how they discard old devices, separating electronic waste completely from general household refuse. Officials also emphasized several safety steps that people should follow carefully before throwing away electronics:

1. Erase personal data. Wipe the data from mobile phones, computers and tablets by performing a factory reset, and remove memory cards and SIM cards. This helps keep personal information and financial transactions from falling into the hands of scammers.

2. Remove the battery. If a device's battery can be taken out, it should be removed before disposal to reduce the risk of sparks, fires or explosions during transport and collection.

3. Use designated drop-off points. Take electronic waste to collection bins, labeled E-Waste, provided by local government bodies, shopping centers or partner organizations. The material is then sent on to recycling plants that meet established standards.

Properly sorting electronic waste, officials said, does more than protect the environment and reduce health risks. It also guards against cyber threats posed by data left on discarded devices.

The agency has invited residents of the lower northeastern region to work together to build better disposal habits, for what it described as the safety of everyone in the community.
