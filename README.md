# The Siam Times
![IMG](IMG_0032.jpeg)
_A 19th-Century Voice Returns to Siam, Now Advocating for the Earth_ 
\Photo: Timelapse Studio

After a 160-year silence sparked by a legal dispute, Thailand’s first English-language newspaper has been reborn as a digital hub for environmental sustainability. The masthead of **The Siam Times**, once a relic of 19th-century journalism, has been reimagined as a digital production platform for the climate era.

In the sweltering July of 1864, a new sound echoed through the streets of Bangkok: the rhythmic clatter of a manual printing press. The Siam Times had arrived, marking the birth of English-language journalism in the kingdom. Its founder, **John Hassett Chandler**, was no mere observer of history; he was a United States Consul and the tutor to King Chulalongkorn, positioning his publication as a vital bridge between a transforming Siam and the Western world.

But the newspaper’s voice was abruptly silenced. In December 1865, Chandler became embroiled in a consular court battle over the importation of steam machinery. In a poignant dispatch to The Straits Times, dated December 4, 1865, he announced a temporary suspension of operations, expressing a fervent hope to return with a revitalized publication once his legal affairs were settled. That return never came. For more than sixteen decades, the paper remained a dormant footnote, its legacy preserved only in the yellowed archives of Thai media history.

Now, that long silence has been broken. The Siam Times has been resurrected, though not on the ink-stained broadsheets of Chandler’s era. Reimagined as an innovative digital media platform, the new iteration is the brainchild of **Montri Kiatphaophan**, Director of Environmental and Pollution Control Office 11 (Nakhon Ratchasima). What began as a personal project to develop modern communication tools morphed into a historical mission when Dr. Kiatphaophan discovered the connection to the defunct 19th-century enterprise.

This modern revival seeks to marry the authoritative weight of the past with the urgent challenges of the present. By securing the trademark for the historic name, the project effectively fulfills Chandler’s 160-year-old promise of a return. Today, instead of reporting on the arrival of steamships, the platform focuses its lens on environmental advocacy and sustainable development, aiming to provide local communities with a global voice in an increasingly fragile world.
