In the Long Wait for a Rolex, a Discovery of the Value in a Casio
For an environmental official in Thailand, trading a luxury status symbol for a utilitarian timepiece became a lesson in the true meaning of public service.
The modest Casio analog watch, featuring a Roman numeral dial, has become a staple for an official who once sought a more prestigious brand.

For many professionals of a certain age and standing, the ritual of the authorized dealer is a rite of passage. In Bangkok, a 50-year-old public servant specializing in environmental management found himself caught in this familiar gravity, standing before a glass case of Rolexes. The allure was visceral—a heavy, ticking testament to a career spent in the upper echelons of government. But as is often the case with the world’s most coveted watches, there was a catch: a formidable waitlist.

What began as a period of anticipation soon transformed into a quiet season of introspection. As he oversaw pollution control across various provinces, the discrepancy between his daily work and the glittering object on his wish list began to sharpen. The high-end status symbol started to feel less like an achievement and more like an encumbrance, clashing with a developing philosophy of minimalism and the grounded realities of his field.

His eyes eventually drifted toward a far more humble alternative: the analog Casio quartz. In the world of horology, the Casio is the antithesis of the "investment piece," yet it offers a triad of virtues that any public servant might envy—affordability, durability, and a dogged reliability. Beyond its mechanics, the watch represented a form of equity. It was a timepiece accessible to the very people he served, a democratic object that mirrored the principles of fairness and equal service.

This was not merely a compromise of convenience; it was a conscious lifestyle pivot. In the rugged conditions of environmental inspections, a luxury watch is a liability; a Casio is an ally. It is easily cleaned, impervious to the elements, and replaceable without heartbreak. His current favorite, a classic model with a Roman numeral dial, possesses an understated elegance that defies its price point, transitioning effortlessly from a muddy site visit to a formal boardroom.

By stepping off the luxury waitlist, he found a different kind of grounding. The choice served as a daily reminder that a person’s legacy is rarely etched in gold or measured by the brand on their wrist. Instead, it is found in the weight of their contributions—to their community, their country, and the environment they work to protect. In the end, the most valuable thing the waitlist gave him was the time to realize he didn't need to be on it.
